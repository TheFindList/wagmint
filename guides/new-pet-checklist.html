New Pet Guide

The new-pet checklist worth saving

Bringing home a pet is easier when the basics are ready before day one. The goal is not to buy everything at once; it is to create a safe, predictable setup and learn what your individual animal actually needs.

1. Confirm species-specific needs

Research appropriate housing, temperature, humidity, diet, enrichment, social needs and exercise for the exact species, age and size of your pet. Advice that works for one animal may be unsafe for another.

2. Prepare a quiet first-day space

Set up food and water, an appropriate resting or hiding area, safe flooring or bedding, and any required habitat equipment before arrival. Keep the first hours calm and limit unnecessary handling while the animal adjusts.

3. Identification and records

Keep adoption or breeder records, vaccination or veterinary documents, medication instructions and emergency contacts together. For species where microchipping or identification tags are appropriate, confirm the information is current.

4. Buy the basics, not a cart full of extras

Start with the essentials: appropriate food, safe feeding equipment, cleaning supplies, secure transport, habitat basics and a few suitable enrichment options. Add accessories after you learn your pet's behavior and preferences.

5. Plan professional care

Know where you will go for routine and urgent care. Exotic pets, birds, reptiles and some small mammals may require a veterinarian with species-specific experience, so identify one before an emergency.

Safety note: This guide is general information, not veterinary advice. Follow qualified veterinary and species-appropriate guidance for health, diet, habitat and medical decisions.
